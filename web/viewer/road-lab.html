the OTHER coast — the Heights set (beachshore1 / beach1 / beachmtnoverlay) the coast north of the mountains is a different set, and landinfo.xml says so by filename: <LandEdge filename="springfieldheightsnobeach" direction="y" culling="left" width="16" offsety="-7" repeat="6">. Three edges, not two: beachshore1 is its water's edge, beach1 is the sand behind it with a rocky cliff/mountain ridge rising inland (its bgColor="EEDDCCFF" is the sand fill), and beachmtnoverlay (repeat="1", one stamp) is the mountain mass where the ridge meets the sea. Two things make this the northern set rather than a third look: the repeat is finite (6 stamps, then stop) where the southern set is -1 (stamp until the edge ends), and its shore art measures the same columns as the southern shore (x -4…0, 17 rows) — so north of the seam it replaces those tiles; it is not a second band inland of them. Art: springfieldheightsnobeach.bsv3 / .png, same pre-sheared oblique model at 64 art px per town tile.

map placement — two coasts, one seam the coast as the viewer models it: sea west of grid x = 0 (terrain.js seaFill −64…0, coast y 0..512), land east of it. The southern (below-the-mountains) coast is one continuous band of the Beach pair, and it stops where the mountain ridge meets the water: landinfo.xml's own land table splits its two beach ranges at y = -16 (<GridTypeRange type="beach" x_start="0" x_end="4" y_start="-16"/> south of it — the 4-tile band the measured buildarea box matches tile for tile — and type="beach" x_end="12" y_end="-16" + type="cliff" x_start="12" x_end="16" y_end="-16" north of it, the wider Heights coast), and the region table puts the mountain band at SHMountains y -18..0 with Springfield Heights above it. That split row is one candidate seam; the default is the other, and it is measured off the save — the game itself puts Mountain03/LowerMountainBeach (16×16) and Mountain04/LowerMountainBeachShoreline (6×16) at x -6…16 with their south edge on row 0, so the ridge foot sits in rows -16…0 at the west end of the Mountains_Standard wall. The two sources bracket the row, so the coast from stepper is the seam: the fill runs from it south to coast to, and the canvas draws both candidate rows (bright dashed = the live seam, faint = the type table's own y = -16) until the map decides. North of the seam the coast is the other set (springfieldheightsnobeach, repeat="6"): heights draws it, mode decides whether it replaces the lower strips north of the seam (both shores measure the same columns, so that is what the file implies) or is painted over them at the same anchors (the stacking question, visible). LandEdge repeat="-1" still means stamp until the edge ends — every edge of a known fixed length carries a finite repeat (the Heights set 6, the MOUNTAINSTOWATER cap 1, Krustyland's fence 13/12), so the southern set really does fill its stretch: 528 tiles ÷ the 17 rows one strip paints = 32 strips (the art reaches 18 tiles but fills only 17 rows, so an 18-tile pitch would leave a 1-row gap every 18). Coverage itself is derived from the opaque art (anims[].tilebox): shore covers the 4 tiles water-side of the anchor column, buildarea the 4 tiles inland — so the anchor column is the shoreline. fit picks the zoom that shows the whole stretch (at ×1 it would be a 34k-px canvas); the zoom stepper turns it off to inspect one section. iso draws through the City View's own projection (CAM.tileAffine, 2:1 oblique; the pre-sheared art blits unshifted because its own tile step is that projection's (+64, +32) px), off gives a flat top-down grid. Still open: the seam row, the repeat phase (the pitch steppers), the anchor column, and what width="16" means (dashed amber — the measured band is only ~8 tiles deep).
